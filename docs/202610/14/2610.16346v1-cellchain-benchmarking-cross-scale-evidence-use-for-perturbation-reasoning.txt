## ABSTRACT 

Recent advances in foundation models and large-scale cellular perturbation datasets have driven growing interest in using LLMs to predict and reason about cellular responses. Biological reasoning in this setting involves relating molecular mech-anisms, cellular responses, and functional outcomes across scales. Evaluating foundation models therefore requires assessing not only whether their predictions are correct, but also how those predictions depend on the biological evidence provided. We introduce CellChain, an experimentally grounded benchmark com-prising 590 biological cases and 2,360 matched evidence-condition instances across gene-dependency prediction, cytokine-axis identification, and drug-sensitivity rank-ing. CellChain links perturbation experiments and external biological resources through a provenance-aware evidence graph spanning molecular, pathway, tran-scriptional, and phenotypic information. Task-specific conditions vary evidence coverage, composition, or assignment while preserving questions, candidate an-swers, reference targets, and scoring rules. Across 18 foundation models, the highest accuracies under prespecified target-evidence conditions are 57.5%, 75.5%, and 70.8%, respectively, and broader evidence coverage does not consistently improve accuracy. On the drug-sensitivity benchmark cohort, matched reference histories outperform swapped histories by an average of 13.0 percentage points in cluster-weighted accuracy. Decision-transition analysis separates corrections, newly introduced errors, and wrong-answer switches, revealing substantial answer changes hidden by small net accuracy differences. CellChain provides controlled comparisons for attributing changes in model predictions to prespecified expan-sions of biological context, including changes in evidence coverage, composition, and biological assignment. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

In recent years, researchers have developed a variety of benchmarks to evaluate large language models’ biological reasoning capabilities, with an emphasis on cellular prediction tasks (Zhang et al., 2026a; Zhao et al., 2026) and pathway-centered mechanistic reasoning (Zhao et al., 2025; Kim et al., 2026). While these benchmarks assess important aspects of biological understanding, they primarily evaluate whether models produce correct outcomes, providing limited insight into how those outcomes depend on the biological information supplied in context. This limitation is particularly important for cellular perturbation reasoning, where models must connect molecular interactions, pathway processes, transcriptional responses, and functional outcomes across biological scales (Bunne et al., 2024). Evaluating biological reasoning therefore requires going beyond endpoint correctness to determine how model decisions change as specific biological evidence is introduced, combined, or reassigned under controlled conditions. Moreover, predictive improvements alone do not establish cross-scale integration, as models may exploit target-associated cues rather than integrate query-relevant experimental evidence. 

To address this gap, we introduce CellChain, an evidence-grounded benchmark for characterizing foundation model predictions under controlled cross-scale biological evidence. CellChain integrates large-scale perturbation experiments with external biological resources through an evidence graph connecting perturbations and cellular contexts to molecular annotations, pathway relations, transcriptional responses, and phenotypic outcomes. Reference answers are defined from experimental measurements or intervention metadata, and model-visible evidence retains its source provenance. Matched evaluation conditions preserve the question, candidate set, reference answer, and scoring rule while varying prespecified evidence-exposure configurations. This enables attribution of prediction changes to the corresponding context interventions while holding the underlying decision task fixed. This design spans genetic, cytokine, and chemical perturbations, supporting comparisons of predictive performance and evidence-dependent decisions without assuming that accuracy identifies the underlying reasoning mechanism. Table 1 situates CellChain relative to prior benchmarks. We evaluate 18 frontier foundation models, spanning proprietary and open-source models. The highest accuracies are 57.5% for Gene Dependency Prediction, 75.5% for Cytokine-Axis Inverse Recovery, and 70.8% for Drug-Sensitivity Ranking, leaving substantial predictive headroom even under the target evidence conditions. Evidence-associated gains vary markedly across tasks and conditions, with broader context failing to yield consistent improvements. Moreover, substantial changes in model predictions can accompany nearly unchanged accuracy, revealing behavior obscured by aggregate performance alone. Model rankings also vary across tasks, with open-source models achieving competitive performance alongside proprietary systems. Together, these findings motivate evaluating both prediction accuracy and evidence-dependent decisions in biological reasoning. 

## 2 CELL CHAIN 

We present CellChain, a benchmark for evaluating foundation-model prediction behavior under controlled cross-scale biological evidence in cellular perturbation reasoning. CellChain has three key features: (1) Cross-scale evidence integration , connecting molecular annotations, pathway relations, transcriptional responses, and phenotypic measurements through a traceable evidence graph (§2.1); (2) Complementary perturbation reasoning tasks , assessing decisions across genetic, cytokine, and chemical perturbations (§2.2); and (3) Controlled evidence exposure , examining how information content and biological associations affect model decisions under paired evaluation conditions (§2.3). The right panel illustrates a representative cytokine-axis evaluation with a held-out reference answer. 2.1 CROSS -SCALE EVIDENCE INTEGRATION 

We integrate three experimental anchor datasets spanning chemical, cytokine, and genetic perturba-tions: Tahoe-100M (Zhang et al., 2026b), PBMC Parse10M (Oesinghaus et al., 2025), and Replogle Perturb-seq (Replogle et al., 2022). These are linked to 14 external resources providing functional endpoints, molecular annotations, pathway knowledge, cellular context, and interaction evidence, including PRISM, DepMap, PubChem, ChEMBL, BindingDB, UniProtKB, Reactome, Gene Ontolo-gy/GOA, Cellosaurus, scBaseCount, CORUM, IntAct, SIGNOR, and Open Targets (Corsello et al., 2020; Tsherniak et al., 2017; Kim et al., 2023; Zdrazil et al., 2024; Gilson et al., 2016; bat, 2025; Milacic et al., 2024; Aleksander et al., 2023; Huntley et al., 2015; Bairoch, 2018; Youngblut et al., 2026; Tsitsiridis et al., 2023; Del Toro et al., 2022; Lo Surdo et al., 2023; Ochoa et al., 2023). Table 5 summarizes their data types and roles in CellChain. All records are represented as provenance-tagged evidence units that preserve source-specific seman-tics. Target-defining endpoint fields are reserved for held-out query construction and scoring, whereas prespecified annotations, measurements, and eligible reference-case evidence may be exposed under task-specific conditions. Held-out query targets and all fields used to derive them are excluded from model inputs, and curated associations are not interpreted as direct causal or experimental effects unless explicitly supported by the source. 

Entity and condition alignment. We align heterogeneous sources using shared biological entities and explicit experimental context. Entity mappings establish cross-source links, while metadata such as cell type or cell line, donor, dose, and duration determine whether linked evidence is applicable to a given case. This preserves source-specific measurement semantics and avoids treating biologically related records as experimentally equivalent. 

Evidence graph organization. We organize cross-scale information into a provenance-aware evidence graph linking perturbations and cellular contexts to task-relevant evidence across biological levels. The evidence graph is a provenance-linked organization of record. For Gene Dependency, CRISPR perturbations are linked to UniProtKB annotations, Reactome pathways, and Perturb-seq transcriptional responses, while DepMap gene-effect measurements remain held out. For Cytokine-Axis Recovery, cytokine-treated cellular contexts are linked to measured transcriptional responses, process-level evidence, and system context, while the applied ligand–receptor axis remains held out. For Drug-Sensitivity Ranking, compound–cell-model contexts are linked to transcriptional responses and development-set reference histories, while the query drug-sensitivity endpoint remains held out. 3Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2027 2.2 BENCHMARK TASK CONSTRUCTION 

CellChain defines three complementary perturbation prediction tasks. Gene Dependency Prediction 

predicts a category derived from held-out DepMap gene-effect scores, which quantify the effect of gene disruption on cell fitness. Cytokine-Axis Inverse Recovery identifies the applied cytokine through a ligand–receptor axis mapped from the recorded stimulus using frozen PBMC annotations. 

Drug-Sensitivity Ranking selects the more sensitive of two cell models exposed to the same compound, using held-out PRISM drug-response AUC as the reference endpoint. 

Selecting Biological Cases. We construct eligible cases by linking perturbation measurements, cellular contexts, and task-specific reference targets. Gene cases require matched response and dependency records; Cytokine cases require treated cells with donor–cell-type-matched controls; Drug cases require complete transcriptional trajectories, QC-qualified sensitivity endpoints, and sufficient development-set reference-history coverage. These criteria ensure that the required model-visible evidence is available while reference targets remain independently defined. Additional case-selection procedures and evidence diagnostics are described in Appendix D. 

Defining Prediction Targets. Gene Dependency uses three categories to distinguish strong, inter-mediate, and weak or absent dependency. Writing g for the gene-effect score, we retain cases with 

g ≤ − 1.05 , −0.75 ≤ g ≤ − 0.55 , or g ≥ − 0.25 , and exclude scores in the intervening intervals as boundary-ambiguous. The Cytokine reference is the recorded stimulus mapped to its ligand–receptor axis using the frozen PBMC annotation resource, rather than an axis inferred from the measured response. For Drug Sensitivity, lower PRISM AUC indicates greater sensitivity, and pairs require 

|∆AUC | ≥ 0.15 to exclude near-tie comparisons. 

Curating QA Tasks. Each case contains a biological question, candidate answers, and a reference label. Gene presents the three dependency categories; Cytokine presents the target axis and three distractors, yielding a common four-choice inverse-identification task; Drug presents two cell models in both candidate orders, so that order-invariance can be assessed independently of task difficulty. Within a case, the question, candidate set, reference target, and scoring rule are held fixed across evidence conditions. This controls the task structure so that paired comparisons reflect the specified change in visible evidence or its assignment, rather than a different question or answer space. 2.3 CONTROLLED EVIDENCE EXPOSURE 

We evaluate evidence dependence through controlled-evidence contrasts between matched biological contexts. Each base case retains the same question, candidate answers, held-out query target, and scoring rule, while the prespecified model-visible evidence or its biological assignment varies. 

Controlling Information Coverage. We construct matched conditions that vary the biological evidence available for reasoning. Cumulative conditions progressively add evidence types and define incremental contrasts under a fixed order. Cytokine conditions present process annotations and system context separately and jointly under a shared evidence budget, while retaining the measured response in every condition. Baseline conditions retain the information required to specify each decision problem. The process annotations are linked to the recorded cytokine stimulus, and the joint condition reallocates the evidence budget between sources. 

Testing Biological Associations. We further vary whether biological evidence is assigned to the entities it describes. Specifically, in Drug-Sensitivity Ranking, matched histories associate each candi-date with development-set phenotype evidence from the same cell model, whereas swapped histories exchange these histories between candidates. To avoid leakage, reference histories are fixed before model evaluation from 36 disjoint development compounds after PRISM quality control (median fit 

R2 ≥ 0.5, replicate AUC range ≤ 0.1, positive slope, and ordered asymptotes); within-compound AUC comparisons determine sensitivity percentiles without using query-compound AUC or query transcriptional responses to select reference records. Both conditions retain the query responses and the same history-comparison instruction. Separating Evidence from Targets. To preserve a clear separation between prediction evidence and scoring targets, held-out query endpoint values and explicit answer keys are excluded from model-visible inputs. Other measurements and reference-case evidence are exposed only when prespecified for the corresponding condition and remain subject to the same task-specific masking rules. In addition, task-specific identity masking is applied consistently across matched conditions, while Cytokine target-linked process annotations remain associated with the recorded stimulus. Finally, model-visible inputs are screened during quality control for prohibited endpoint fields, answer labels, direct target-revealing information, and condition-specific leakage cues. 2.4 DATASET ANALYSIS 

Table 2 summarizes the construction and coverage of CellChain. The benchmark comprises 590 problems derived from 17 biological resources spanning genetic, cytokine, and chemical pertur-bations, with evidence across molecular, pathway-process, transcriptional, and phenotypic levels. Experimental contexts include cell type, cell line, donor, dose, and duration where applicable. We apply four quality-control checks to each benchmark case. First, cross-source links must agree on the biological entity and cellular context, and unresolved mappings are excluded. Second, reference answers are recomputed from the underlying experimental endpoint or recorded intervention identity using the prespecified task rule; only cases with a unique valid answer are retained. Third, the question, candidate set, reference answer, and scoring rule remain fixed across matched evidence con-ditions, with only the specified evidence or associated instructions changing. Finally, model-visible inputs are screened for answer labels, prohibited query endpoint fields, and task-specific identity strings. Cases failing an applicable check in any condition are excluded as matched units. Six PhD researchers manually reviewed 150 sampled cases, of which 142 passed (94.7%).

Cross-scale biological reasoning remains challenging for current foundation models. Across evaluated foundation models, the highest accuracies under the prespecified target-evidence conditions are 57.5% for Gene Dependency, 75.5% for Cytokine Axis, and 70.8% for Drug Sensitivity (Table 3a). The corresponding error rates under the task-specific scoring rules are 42.5%, 24.5%, and 29.2%. The three tasks have different candidate counts and should not be ranked by raw accuracy alone; their uniform-choice levels are 33.3%, 25.0%, and 50.0%. Within each task, accuracy spans 32.5– 57.5%, 25.5–75.5%, and 33.3–70.8%, showing that the observed headroom is accompanied by substantial differences in model capability rather than a single shared performance ceiling. In particular, several models remain near uniform-choice on Gene Dependency despite receiving the full cumulative evidence, whereas stronger results on Cytokine Axis and Drug Sensitivity show the corresponding decision problems contain usable predictive information. The performance gap between proprietary and open-source foundation models varies by biological task. Proprietary models achieve the highest observed accuracy on Gene Dependency and Cytokine Axis, whereas open-source models lead on Drug Sensitivity. On Gene Dependency, GPT-5.6-Sol reaches 57.5%, compared with 55.0% for the strongest open-source model, GLM-5.3. On Cytokine Axis, Gemini-3.8-Flash obtains 75.5%, followed closely by Qwen3.8-Flash-Next at 74.5%. The ordering reverses on Drug Sensitivity, where GPT-OSS-20B and Qwen3.8-27B achieve 70.8% and 70.6%, respectively, compared with 70.2% for Gemini-3.8-Flash. These differences are descriptive rankings rather than evidence of statistically significant differences between model groups. More broadly, the variation in task leaders indicates that performance differences between proprietary and open-source systems are task dependent rather than consistent across CellChain. These reversals highlight the value of CellChain’ complementary task design in revealing task-specific capability differences obscured by aggregate model rankings. 

Small net accuracy changes conceal substantial decision turnover. Figure 3 decomposes predic-tion changes into corrections, newly introduced errors, wrong-answer switches, and invalid-related transitions. Gene Pathway-to-Transcriptional changes 9.5% of predictions for a −0.4-point net difference, Cytokine Response-to-System changes 24.1% for −0.3 points, and Drug Swapped-to-Matched changes 75.1% for a +13 .0-point gain. In each case, substantially more individual decisions change than the corresponding net accuracy difference suggests, because improvements can be offset by newly introduced errors or switches among incorrect answers. The positive panel-level Drug assignment effect also masks substantial model heterogeneity, revealing model-specific sensitivity to biological evidence assignment beyond aggregate accuracy. Additional diagnostics reveal sensitivity beyond aggregate accuracy: under Gene Base, all foundation models produce a single predicted category, while under Drug Matched, reversing candidate order changes 20.1% of predictions de-spite preserving the biological target. Invalid outputs remain rare overall (187/59,760, 0.31%). Together, these results show that endpoint accuracy alone can obscure how model decisions respond to controlled evidence interventions and task-preserving presentation changes. 

## 4 CONCLUSION 

We present CellChain, a benchmark for evaluating foundation-model prediction behavior under controlled cross-scale biological evidence. Across 18 models, the highest accuracies are 57.5% for Gene Dependency, 75.5% for Cytokine Axis, and 70.8% for Drug Sensitivity, leaving substantial predictive headroom. Evidence effects are selective: target-linked process annotations yield the largest Cytokine gains, matched reference histories outperform swapped histories on average in Drug Sensitivity, while additional transcriptional or system context provides little net benefit. Paired analyses further show that small accuracy differences can mask substantial prediction turnover. ## A RELATED WORK 

A.1 AI V IRTUAL CELL DATASETS AND MODELS 

AI virtual cells seek to represent cellular states and predict their responses across perturbations and contexts (Bunne et al., 2024). Large perturbation resources, including genome-scale Perturb-seq, scPerturb, and Tahoe-100M, provide experimentally grounded intervention–response measure-ments (Replogle et al., 2022; Peidli et al., 2024; Zhang et al., 2026b). Building on these data, single-cell foundation models learn transferable representations (Theodoris et al., 2023; Cui et al., 2024; Hao et al., 2024), while CPA, CellOT, GEARS, STATE, and TxPert model perturbation-induced state transitions using latent, transport-based, graph-based, or set-level architectures (Lotfollahi et al., 2023; Bunne et al., 2023; Roohani et al., 2024; Adduri et al., 2026; Wenkel et al., 2026). Language-oriented approaches such as Cell2Sentence and VCWorld further connect cellular measurements with natural-language or knowledge-grounded reasoning (Levine et al., 2024; Wei et al., 2026). A.2 BIOLOGICAL REASONING BENCHMARKS FOR LLM S

Recent benchmarks evaluate LLMs on increasingly biological forms of cellular reasoning. SOAR focuses on cell-type annotation (Liu et al., 2025); CellVerse and SC-Arena cover annotation, gen-eration, perturbation prediction, and scientific question answering (Zhang et al., 2026a; Zhao et al., 2026); and PerturbQA and VCWorld’s GeneTAK evaluate differential-expression and direction prediction for unseen perturbations (Wu et al., 2025; Wei et al., 2026). BioMaze and PertReasonQA further assess pathway reasoning and the mechanistic faithfulness of perturbation explanations (Zhao et al., 2025; Kim et al., 2026), while AssayBench extends evaluation to phenotypic CRISPR-screen prediction (De Brouwer et al., 2026).

Gene Dependency Prediction Prompt 

SYSTEM 

You are a molecular and cellular biology expert. Evaluate the biological question using only the supplied observations and visible evidence, and select the best-supported answer from the candidates. Entity names, accessions, aliases, and database identifiers may be masked. Do not attempt to recover the hidden entity identity using external knowledge, use hidden labels, or invent unavailable dependency measurements. Return exactly one valid JSON object conforming to the required output schema, without additional explanation or formatting. 

USER 

Question 

Infer the held-out gene-fitness dependency endpoint category for an anonymous CRISPRi perturba-tion in K562. 

Task Requirements

## D EVIDENCE ATTRIBUTION 

The controlled-evidence analysis relies on task-specific evidence constructions and paired evaluation conditions. We therefore report additional details on case eligibility, benchmark selection, and evidence construction, together with the condition-level measurements underlying the contrasts in the main text. 

Case Eligibility and Benchmark Selection. Across tasks, a case is considered eligible only when its biological entity and experimental context can be aligned, the required task-specific measurements and evidence are available, and the reference target maps to a unique candidate answer. The question, candidate set, reference target, and scoring rule are then held fixed across matched evidence conditions, with prohibited query-endpoint fields excluded from model-visible inputs. Failure of any applicable criterion excludes the corresponding case and all of its matched conditions. Source-record and candidate-case counts use different units and therefore do not define a direct attrition sequence. For Gene Dependency, 2,448 endpoint-labeled candidates entered the evidence-availability and boundary-margin checks, of which 2,092 satisfied the eligibility criteria. We then sampled 40 cases from each dependency-category–cell-line stratum to construct the 240-case bench-mark set. For Cytokine Axis, 267 cases satisfied the task-specific eligibility criteria, from which 110 cases were selected with ten cases per target axis. For Drug Sensitivity, 737 candidate pairs satisfied the curve-quality, complete-dose-trajectory, and AUC-separation criteria; 240 pairs were selected for benchmark evaluation to increase compound coverage while limiting reuse of drug–cell-model units. Differences between eligible and benchmark case counts therefore reflect benchmark-set construction rather than additional case-level quality-control failures. The frozen construction artifacts do not provide mutually exclusive counts for every earlier rejection criterion, so we do not further partition the remaining source records by failure reason. 

Cytokine Process Evidence. The Cytokine conditions retain the measured transcriptional response while varying the additional process and system context. Process annotations are linked to the recorded cytokine stimulus, whereas system evidence describes the corresponding cellular context. The Process–Response contrast therefore measures the predictive value associated with the target-linked process context supplied to the model. Response–mechanism integration is not separately identified by this contrast. The Joint condition combines Process and System evidence under the same overall evidence budget by reallocating the available evidence units between the two sources. Accordingly, 

Γ = AJoint − AProcess − ASystem + AResponse 

is treated as a joint-condition deviation from additivity under this budget constraint. 

Drug Reference-History Construction. Drug reference histories are constructed from 36 develop-ment compounds that are disjoint from the query compounds. Development records are retained after PRISM quality control requiring median fit R2 ≥ 0.5, replicate AUC range ≤ 0.1, positive slope, and ordered asymptotes. Within each development compound, relative sensitivity is represented by within-compound AUC-based percentiles for the corresponding cell models. Query-compound AUC values and query transcriptional responses are not used to construct or select these reference-history records. The Matched and Swapped conditions contain the same query-response information and use the same history-comparison instruction. In the Matched condition, each reference history remains associated with the cell model from which it was derived; in the Swapped condition, the histories are exchanged between the two candidates. The Matched–Swapped contrast therefore measures sensitivity to candidate–history assignment conditional on the supplied query and reference-history evidence. 

Drug Reference-History Signal Diagnostic. After selecting the 240-case Drug benchmark cohort, we evaluated whether its development-set reference histories contained predictive signal using a simple evidence-only rule. For each candidate cell model, the rule averages its sensitivity percentiles across available development-compound histories and selects the candidate with the higher mean. Predictions are scored against the held-out query label, and accuracy is averaged equally across the 26 19 Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2027 query compounds. The rule attains 64.2% compound-weighted accuracy, exceeding the prespecified 60% cohort-level threshold. The diagnostic is applied to the fixed benchmark cohort and does not remove individual cases. Because its evaluation uses held-out query labels after benchmark-set selection, we interpret the resulting accuracy as a post-selection measure of reference-history informativeness. The Drug evidence contrasts reported in the main text therefore characterize the benchmark cohort rather than all 737 eligible candidate pairs. 

Condition-Level Measurements. Table 6 reports biological-cluster-weighted accuracy under each evidence condition. These values provide the condition-level measurements underlying the controlled-evidence contrasts in Table 3b. Gene Dependency contrasts weight perturbed genes equally, Cytokine Axis contrasts weight cytokine axes equally, and Drug Sensitivity contrasts weight query compounds equally. For Drug Sensitivity, both candidate-order presentations remain paired within each case, and invalid outputs receive zero score under the fixed denominator. ## E BIOLOGICAL -C LUSTER BOOTSTRAP CONFIDENCE INTERVALS 

We estimate uncertainty using 10,000 biological-cluster bootstrap replicates with the task-specific clusters defined in Section 3.2. In each replicate, clusters are sampled with replacement while retaining all associated cases, candidate-order presentations, evidence conditions, and model predictions jointly. For Drug Sensitivity, both candidate-order presentations of each case remain within the same compound cluster. Endpoint accuracy averages presentation scores within each case and weights cases equally, whereas controlled contrasts weight biological clusters equally. We report percentile-based 95% confidence intervals using the 2.5th and 97.5th percentiles of the bootstrap distribution. Table 7 reports model-level case-weighted endpoint accuracies and their 95% confidence intervals under the prespecified target-evidence conditions. Table 8 reports the corresponding aggregate controlled-evidence effects across the 18-model panel, including incremental, joint-deviation, and assignment contrasts. The latter averages paired case-score differences within biological clusters and then averages the resulting model-level effects across the fixed model panel. 20 Published as a conference paper at ICLR 2027 These intervals are marginal under a single perturbation-clustering scheme. Shared donors, controls, cell models, and reference histories may induce dependence across clusters, and the intervals do not establish robustness to such crossed dependence. The model panel is fixed, so they do not quantify uncertainty over model families or future models. Intervals containing zero should not be interpreted as evidence of equivalence.
