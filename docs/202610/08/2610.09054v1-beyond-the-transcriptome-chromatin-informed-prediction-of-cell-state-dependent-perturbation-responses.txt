## ABSTRACT 

Predicting transcriptional responses to genetic perturbations is central to under-standing gene function. Existing predictors primarily rely on transcriptomic mea-surements, although chromatin accessibility provides complementary information about the cellular context in which perturbations act. Using this information requires linking chromatin context to specific perturbations and accounting for baseline differences between independently sampled control and perturbed popu-lations. We propose ChromaPert , a chromatin-informed framework for predict-ing state-dependent perturbation responses. ChromaPert combines molecular and DNA-informed locus priors with paired control RNA–ATAC features to jointly represent target identity and measured cellular context. Its Chromatin-Guided Re-sponse Router (CGRR) builds a source bank of control-derived expression offsets and retrieves relevant sources using perturbation/locus or control-ATAC similar-ity. These offsets account for baseline differences, while conditional transport flow learns the remaining response. Across three unseen-target and held-out-state set-tings on K562 CAT-ATAC and Perturb-Multiome, ChromaPert achieves the high-est all-gene response correlation among evaluated methods. When transferring known perturbations to held-out states, it improves correlation by 23.2% across all genes and 46.2% for the twenty most responsive genes over the strongest re-spective baselines. Correct RNA–ATAC pairing improves recovery of chromatin-associated response slopes over shuffled pairing, while cross-state predictions re-tain state-specific responses to the same perturbation. 

## 1 INTRODUCTION 

Predicting transcriptional responses to perturbations is central to functional genomics and gene-function characterization. Single-cell CRISPR and combinatorial screens provide large-scale mea-surements (Dixit et al., 2016; Norman et al., 2019), but their coverage remains incomplete across perturbations and cellular contexts. This gap motivates in silico prediction for unseen perturbations and cellular backgrounds. Most perturbation predictors represent cellular state using transcriptomic profiles from single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq), which measures RNA abundance as a readout of gene expression. They learn expression shifts, exploit gene relationships, or model mappings from control to per-turbed cells (Lotfollahi et al., 2019; 2023; Roohani et al., 2024; Bunne et al., 2023; Klein et al., 2025). Yet transcriptomic state alone may miss regulatory context encoded by chromatin acces-sibility —the physical accessibility of DNA packaged with proteins. Single-cell ATAC sequencing (scATAC-seq) measures this complementary signal (Buenrostro et al., 2015), which may improve perturbation prediction in two ways. First, accessibility at response- relevant regulatory regions can influence transcription-factor recruitment and downstream transcription; for example, it shapes glucocorticoid-receptor binding after hormone stimulation (John et al., 2011; Biddie et al., 2011). 1arXiv:2610.09054v1 [q-bio.GN] 6 Oct 2026 Preprint Second, it can distinguish cellular backgrounds with similar RNA profiles but different regulatory states, as observed in paired RNA–ATAC measurements of mouse skin, where basal-cell popula-tions showed distinct accessibility patterns (Hao et al., 2021). Together, these findings motivate chromatin-informed modeling of perturbation responses. Using chromatin for perturbation prediction raises two challenges. i) How can the RNA–chromatin background be linked to the response to a specific perturbation? Control RNA–ATAC mea-surements describe gene expression and chromatin accessibility in unperturbed cells, whereas the prediction target is the expression change associated with a specific perturbation. The model must connect the RNA–chromatin background with the perturbation and learn how they jointly shape the transcriptional response. However, because single-cell profiling is typically destructive, this re-lationship must be learned from separately sampled control and perturbed populations rather than longitudinal before–after measurements of the same cells (Bunne et al., 2023). This intro-duces the second challenge: ii) How can pre-existing expression differences be distinguished from perturbation-associated changes in these unpaired populations? Control and perturbed populations may have different expression baselines. When reference controls do not represent the appropriate baseline, particularly when state-specific perturbed expression is compared with con-trols pooled across states, the observed difference can contain both pre-existing baseline shifts and perturbation-associated changes. The model must therefore account for these control-derived base-line differences rather than learning them as perturbation responses. To address these challenges, we introduce ChromaPert , a chromatin-informed framework for pre-dicting perturbation expression (Figure 1). To link cellular background to the specific perturbation, its chromatin-informed perturbation condition combines molecular and DNA-informed locus priors with paired control RNA–ATAC features. Protein and RNA-token embeddings encode target identity, while promoter-sequence and regulatory annotations describe its locus context. These com-plementary priors relate unseen targets to training perturbations; control RNA–ATAC features spec-ify the cellular background in which they act. To account for baseline differences, its Chromatin-Guided Response Router (CGRR) builds a source bank of control-derived expression offsets. It routes queries by perturbation/locus similarity for unseen-target prediction or control-ATAC sim-ilarity for cross-state transfer. This decomposition separates baseline variation from the response learned by conditional transport flow, which reconstructs post-perturbation expression relative to matched controls for each perturbation in its corresponding cellular background. ChromaPert achieves the highest all-gene response correlation among evaluated methods in unseen-target prediction on K562 CAT-ATAC (Shevade et al., 2025) and Perturb-Multiome (Martin-Rufino et al., 2025), and in cross-state transfer on the latter. Relative held-out-state gains reach 23.2% 

across all genes and 46.2% for the twenty most responsive genes over the strongest respective base-lines (Table 3). Ablations support the model components; RNA–ATAC pairing and cross-state analy-ses show preserved chromatin-associated and state-dependent response structure (Table 4; Figure 2). Our main contributions are summarized as follows: • Chromatin-informed perturbation conditioning. We integrate molecular and DNA-informed locus priors with paired control RNA–ATAC features, allowing the perturbation condition to adapt to the measured RNA–chromatin context. • Chromatin-guided response routing. CGRR builds response sources from control-derived ex-pression offsets and performs query-dependent routing, separating pre-existing baseline variation from the response subsequently modeled by conditional transport flow. • Predictive and response-structure validation. ChromaPert improves perturbation-response pre-diction across unseen-perturbation and cross-state settings, while controlled ATAC and response-structure analyses support the predictive value of chromatin-informed conditioning.

Generative modeling of unpaired responses. Generative models, including diffusion models, predict responses to unseen interventions (Qi et al., 2024; Chi et al., 2025a; Yuan et al., 2026; Ruan et al., 2026). CellFlow uses flow matching, and scDFM adds distribution-level alignment (Klein et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2026). Conditional flow matching parameterizes transport between distribu-tions (Lipman et al., 2022), but its learning target depends on the response definition and control reference. ChromaPert defines responses relative to a pooled training-control reference, subtracts a source component derived from control-state offsets, and fits a train-only conditional transport flow to the remaining response. Its source-on candidate combines the routed source component with the decoded transport-flow displacement before reliability gating and matched-control reconstruction. 

Chromatin-aware and multimodal modeling. Chromatin accessibility complements RNA-derived cell state (Buenrostro et al., 2015; Ma et al., 2020). ArchR, weighted-neighbor analy-sis, and CLM-X integrate regulatory or multimodal information (Granja et al., 2021; Hao et al., 2021; Li et al., 2026); Perturb-Multiome and CAT-ATAC provide paired perturbation profiles (Martin-Rufino et al., 2025; Shevade et al., 2025). CellOracle uses multimodal regulatory networks to simulate transcription-factor perturbations (Kamimoto et al., 2023). ChromaPert uses target/locus or control-ATAC similarity, according to the evaluation setting, to route each query to expression offsets derived from training control states. These control-derived offsets, not known perturbation effects, form the source prior for conditional transport-flow prediction.

## 4 EXPERIMENTS 

4.1 EXPERIMENTAL SETUP 

Datasets and protocols. We use paired RNA–ATAC from Perturb-Multiome (Martin-Rufino et al., 2025) (GSE274113; 19 targets across differentiating hematopoietic states) and CAT-ATAC (Shevade et al., 2025) (GSE288996; 18 targets in DMSO-treated K562). Both test unseen targets; GSE also evaluates 19 known targets across seven held-out states (133 conditions). Supplied test cell-type counts weight population means (Appendix B.4). Data composition, preprocessing, splits, coupling, and query controls are detailed in Appendices B.1–B.2. 

Comparison methods. The 14 learned baselines comprise (1) graph-based models : GEARS ,

CellOracle , and GraphVCI (Roohani et al., 2024; Kamimoto et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2022); (2) 

foundation models : scGPT , scFoundation , GeneCompass , CellFM , and CellVQ (Cui et al., 2024; Hao et al., 2024; Yang et al., 2024; Zeng et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2026); and (3) generative models : CPA , CellFlow , scDFM , PerturbDiff , DeSCOPE , and STATE (Lotfollahi et al., 2023; Klein et al., 2025; Yu et al., 2026; Yuan et al., 2026; Wu et al., 2026; Adduri et al., 2026). scGPT uses its native perturbation decoder; other foundation models use frozen embeddings with a GEARS-style adapter. Training and prediction details appear in Appendix B.6. 

Metrics and reporting. Methods share a gene panel and evaluator; unperturbed Control is ex-cluded from learned-model rankings. Pearson ∆ correlates condition-mean responses across genes relative to matched controls; Pearson ∆,DE uses the 20 largest absolute observed responses. L2,MSE, and MAE measure mean-expression error, discriminative cosine measures direction, and sup-plementary W1 evaluates exported predictions (Appendix B.6). Scores average held-out conditions (four decimals); see Appendices B.5 and A.3 for definitions and three-seed summaries. 4.2 SINGLE -CELL -LINE UNSEEN -TARGET PERTURBATION PREDICTION

## 5 CONCLUSION 

ChromaPert separates pooled baseline offsets from target-specific perturbation responses and con-ditions transport on paired control RNA–ATAC context. Its structured target/locus conditioning and chromatin-guided routing preserve state-dependent response structure, while conditional flow models the remaining displacement. This decomposition lets the same perturbation acquire distinct response representations across cellular contexts without treating pre-existing control differences as perturbation effects. Together, these components support expression prediction for unseen pertur-bations and transfer of known perturbations to new cellular states within a common formulation for learning from unpaired cell populations, making measured chromatin context directly actionable for response prediction across diverse cellular backgrounds and targets. 9Preprint

B.6 COMPARATOR IMPLEMENTATIONS AND PREDICTION INTERFACES 

All comparators receive the same canonical condition identifiers and gene adapter. RNA and promoter-ATAC coordinates are mapped before scoring; extra genes are dropped and missing canon-ical genes are handled by the method’s fixed adapter while the evaluator dimension remains fixed. Comparators use method-specific preprocessing and optimization schedules from their public im-plementations or run configurations, with adaptations specified below. Fold-specific fits are inde-pendent. Released foundation checkpoints remain frozen, and downstream adapters are trained on fit-fold expression. 

Prediction outputs and scoring. All point-estimation metrics use condition means. GEARS, the frozen-foundation adapters, CPA, and the cross-state CellFlow+ATAC export provide condition means; for W1, these means are repeated to the required number of scoring rows. For methods that export cell samples, W1 uses those samples, while point-estimation metrics use their means. Thus, W1 compares method-specific output representations, whereas the primary metrics compare condition means. 22 Preprint 

Control. Control uses unperturbed expression without a learned perturbation adjustment. Its re-ported scores provide an empirical control-reference comparison and are excluded from best/second-best learned-model highlighting. 

GEARS. GEARS (Roohani et al., 2024) uses its published gene-relation graph and perturbation MLP to predict post-perturbation expression. We followed the official optimizer, preprocessing, and checkpoint rule with the published architecture, and mapped the result to the canonical panel. 

CellOracle and GraphVCI. CellOracle (Kamimoto et al., 2023) uses the published regulatory-network and vector-field simulation workflow. GraphVCI (Wu et al., 2022) uses its graph-based variational causal-inference predictor. We used the public implementations with their default net-work construction, hidden dimensions, and regularization, and fixed graph resources before each split. Both methods produce condition-level gene responses after the supplied gene mapping, with graph construction restricted to fit-fold resources. 

scGPT. scGPT (Cui et al., 2024) is evaluated with its native TransformerGenerator per-turbation decoder, following the official repository protocol, default preprocessing, and checkpoint rule. Generated outputs are mapped to the canonical RNA panel before residualization. 

scFoundation, GeneCompass, CellFM, and CellVQ. scFoundation (Hao et al., 2024), GeneCompass (Yang et al., 2024), CellFM (Zeng et al., 2025), and CellVQ (Wang et al., 2026) provide frozen gene representations. A canonical GEARS-style downstream adapter converts each representation to a condition mean, followed by the same gene mapping and residual evaluator. We trained the downstream adapter for 15 epochs for scFoundation (learning rate 10 −2), 15 epochs for CellFM (learning rate 5 × 10 −3), and 5 epochs for CellVQ (learning rate 5 × 10 −4). GeneCompass uses the same frozen-embedding bridge with the optimizer settings of its public GEARS integration. 

CPA. CPA (Lotfollahi et al., 2023) uses its context-aware perturbation autoencoder and latent in-tervention predictor, trained with the default architecture and optimization settings in the public implementation, then decodes the predicted expression into the canonical panel. The exported pre-diction is the decoded condition mean. 

STATE. STATE (Adduri et al., 2026) uses its published latent transition model and basal-sampled cell-set prediction interface. We trained it from scratch with the public implementation because the released transition checkpoints target different dosage scenarios. The generated set is mapped to the canonical panel. GSE cross-state-LOO reports STATE as unavailable under the frozen export. 

CellFlow, scDFM, PerturbDiff, and DeSCOPE. CellFlow (Klein et al., 2025) is evaluated with the reference flow-matching implementation, including its 50-dimensional PCA latent and ODE in-tegration. scDFM (Yu et al., 2026), PerturbDiff (Yuan et al., 2026), and DeSCOPE (Wu et al., 2026) use their published inference interfaces and released checkpoints/configurations, with adaptation restricted to the fit fold. These methods export generated cell samples, which are mapped to the canonical panel. 

CellFlow+ATAC ablation. The ATAC-augmented CellFlow control applies train-only PCA 50 to the RNA input and train-only SVD 64 to the gene-aligned ATAC PROM input, concatenates the two representations, and passes the result to the native CellFlow predictor. This design tests direct ATAC concatenation while retaining native CellFlow response generation. The GSE cross-state export contains the condition-level prediction.

Configuration and fold construction. Table 13 gives the primary ChromaPert configuration for the GSE protocols. Each fold holds out one target or state and fits the model using the remaining targets and permitted controls. PCA is fitted once on outer-training control RNA and then fixed. Target embeddings are frozen; condition normalization, source summaries, and CGRR routes are fold-specific. Preprocessing and controls follow Appendices B.1–B.2. 

Evaluation-setting instantiation. ChromaPert uses the same pooled-reference decomposition and CGRR source bank in every setting. A conditional transport flow is fit on source-adjusted deltas, a state-expanded transport branch is available when state summaries are used, and Target-LOO ap-plies a train-only reliability gate to the decoded candidate bases; Cross-State uses the statebase branch directly. Unseen-target settings use the target/locus context route to infer a training-state mixture. Held-out-state transfer uses clean-control ATAC to match the query to the training pro-totypes. When population composition is supplied, state-specific predictions are aggregated with those counts; state-transfer evaluation returns the selected state prediction. 

Velocity network and optimization. The velocity MLP concatenates zt, t, and the pooled or state-aware condition c (576 or 736 dimensions, respectively). LayerNorm precedes two width-288 hidden linear layers, each with GELU and dropout, followed by a linear 128-dimensional velocity output. AdamW minimizes Equation 12 for the setting-specific epoch budgets at a constant learning rate. Direction/norm and supervised ATAC loss weights are zero. Only the transport velocity MLPs are trained; PCA and the source bank remain fixed.

## D DISCUSSION AND LIMITATIONS 

ChromaPert connects perturbation targets to cellular regulatory context through chromatin-aware conditioning and source-routed conditional transport flow modeling. Across the three evaluation set-tings, it achieves the highest all-gene response correlation across the benchmark methods. Ablations 25 Preprint show the complementary value of molecular target priors and regulatory context, while biological analyses show that predictions retain chromatin-associated and state-resolved response structure. The main limitation is the requirement for paired scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq control profiles with a compatible promoter-accessibility mapping, restricting direct application when matched multimodal controls are unavailable. The framework predicts post-perturbation expression, with population readouts conditional on supplied state proportions.
