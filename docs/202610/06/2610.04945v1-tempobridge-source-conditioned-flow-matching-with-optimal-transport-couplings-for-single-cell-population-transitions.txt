Abstract 

Destructive single-cell measurements provide unpaired population snapshots rather than observations of the same cells across conditions. Local cell states and transition requests may also be insufficient to distinguish responses across source populations. We introduce TempoBridge, a common source-conditioned transport formulation for temporal, genetic, and chemical population transitions. Source cells initialize latent transport and provide a fixed empirical population summary. The velocity field receives this summary alongside the evolving cell state, flow time, and a structured transition descriptor. Minibatch optimal transport (OT) supplies couplings only for conditional flow-matching training paths; inference requires neither target expression nor OT computation. On held-out donors, TempoBridge achieves an Energy distance of 0.129 versus 0.144 for scGen. Genetic mean-expression L2 error is 2.261 versus 3.156 for scGPT-scratch under Seen 2/2. On held-out compounds, condition-averaged drug-effect correlation is 0.598 versus 0.561 for the CellFlow adapter. Temporal ablations show higher mean distributional error after removing source context, replacing optimal transport with random pairing, or replacing flow matching with static residual regression. Together, these results demonstrate the predictive utility of a common source-conditioned transport formulation across held-out donors, gene combinations, and compounds. 

# 1 Introduction 

Single-cell experiments measure populations at selected time points and under selected perturbations, leaving many differentiation states, gene combinations, and drug responses unobserved (Cuomo et al., 2020; Norman et al., 2019; Srivatsan et al., 2020). We consider the prediction of a target gene-expression distribution from source-cell profiles and a requested time point or perturbation. Because sequencing destroys measured cells, training provides unpaired source and target populations rather than trajectories of the same cells (Schiebinger et al., 2019). At inference, the requested target population is unavailable. Methods for this setting use learned representations of cells and perturbations, mappings between populations, or conditional transport. scGen and CPA model perturbation effects in latent spaces (Lotfollahi et al., 2019; 2023); CellOT maps unpaired populations (Bunne et al., 2023); and CMonge and CellFlow condition transport on experimental descriptors (Driessen et al., 2026; Klein et al., 2025). scDFM uses control-cell expression, while SCALE incorporates a set-level control representation (Yu et al., 2026; Chen 1arXiv:2610.04945v1 [cs.LG] 4 Oct 2026 et al., 2026). These methods establish the value of source cells and experimental conditions. Here we ask how a transport field should use information about the particular source population as its cells move. Initializing a particle from a source cell gives the field that cell’s state, but does not separately expose the population from which it was sampled. The distinction becomes consequential when cells in similar states require different responses across source populations. Under a specified coupling and training-path construction, two populations can have different conditional mean velocities at the same latent state, flow time, and transition descriptor. A field conditioned only on these inputs receives the same input in both cases and cannot represent both velocities. We call this pointwise conflict source-dependent transition ambiguity (Section 3.2). Supplying a source-population summary can resolve the conflict when that summary retains the relevant difference between populations. We introduce TempoBridge, a transport model that keeps an empirical summary of the initial source population available throughout integration. Each source cell initializes a latent particle; mean pooling the initial cell embeddings produces a summary shared by all particles from that population. The velocity field takes the evolving particle state, flow time, source summary, and transition descriptor as separate inputs. We train it with optimal-transport conditional flow matching (OT–CFM), using minibatch Sinkhorn couplings to construct paths between unpaired endpoints (Lipman et al., 2023; Tong et al., 2024). Prediction starts from source-cell latents and requires neither target expression nor OT computation. The temporal, genetic, and drug-response models follow this formulation but are trained independently. We evaluate the complete models on held-out donors, gene combinations, and compounds. For interme-diate populations of unseen donors, TempoBridge achieves an Energy distance of 0.129 versus 0.144 for scGen. On Seen 2/2 gene combinations, mean-expression L2 error is 2.261 versus 3.156 for scGPT-scratch. On compounds excluded from training and validation, condition-averaged drug-effect correlation is 0.598 versus 0.561 for the CellFlow adapter. These comparisons measure the performance of the complete task models, but do not isolate the contribution of source context. Targeted analyses separate source-context use from predictive benefit. In a controlled ambiguity task, context raises branch accuracy from 50.0% to 98.0%; donor-context swaps show that a fitted temporal model uses the summary. Matched temporal retraining lowers Energy from 0.139 to 0.135 and MMD from 0.397 to 0.378 with context, although effect metrics favor no context. Random pairing raises temporal Energy from 0.1291 to 0.1446; a static residual variant reaches 0.1350 but also changes the training objective. A separate drug conditioning analysis finds small gains in effect metrics but no consistent distributional benefit. Our contributions are: • A source-dependent ambiguity. Under fixed couplings and paths, we identify a pointwise conflict for a velocity field given only cell state, flow time, and transition descriptor, and demonstrate it in a controlled construction. • A source-conditioned transport field. A fixed summary of the initial source population conditions each particle’s velocity throughout transport; OT couplings construct training paths without being required at inference. • An assessment of the design. Donor-context swaps and matched context ablations distinguish use from metric-dependent benefit; temporal component variants test the training recipe, while independently trained models are evaluated on held-out donors, gene combinations, and compounds. 

# 2 Related Work 

Optimal transport and flow matching. Optimal transport provides a distributional framework for learning from unpaired cell populations. CellOT learns neural mappings between cellular distributions (Bunne et al., 22023), while CMonge conditions transport mappings on treatment covariates (Driessen et al., 2026). Flow matching learns continuous transformations through velocity regression (Lipman et al., 2023). OT–CFM uses minibatch transport couplings to construct training paths (Tong et al., 2024), while GENOT uses flow matching to learn entropic Wasserstein and Gromov–Wasserstein transport plans (Klein et al., 2024). For cellular response prediction, CellFlow incorporates experimental conditions into flow-based transport (Klein et al., 2025), and scDFM combines control-conditioned generation with MMD-based distributional alignment (Yu et al., 2026). TempoBridge builds on OT–CFM and represents the evolving cell state, empirical source-population context, and structured transition descriptor as separate inputs to the velocity field. Source cells initialize the latent particles, while a fixed summary of their initial population conditions the field throughout integration. 

Temporal population modeling. Temporal single-cell models describe changes in population distri-butions from snapshots collected at different times. Waddington-OT estimates temporal population cou-plings (Schiebinger et al., 2019); PRESCIENT learns potential-based stochastic differentiation dynamics (Yeo et al., 2021). TrajectoryNet uses dynamic optimal transport (Tong et al., 2020), scNODE combines latent representations with neural differential equations (Zhang et al., 2024), and BranchSBM models branching populations through multiple velocity fields and growth processes (Tang et al., 2026). TempoBridge uses endpoint supervision to predict intermediate population distributions at requested times for held-out donors. 

Genetic and chemical perturbation prediction. Predicting responses to unseen interventions requires representations of the requested perturbations. GEARS uses gene-relation graphs for multigene response prediction (Roohani et al., 2024); chemCPA encodes molecular information for novel-compound predic-tion (Hetzel et al., 2022); and MAP combines knowledge-informed representations with a pretrained single-cell model (Feng et al., 2026). Genetic-perturbation benchmarks also motivate strong linear and additive baselines (Ahlmann-Eltze et al., 2025). TempoBridge represents genetic interventions as unordered gene sets and chemical interventions through molecular and experimental descriptors.

# 4 TempoBridge 

For each task q, a shared encoder maps unpaired source and target cells into a latent space. Source latents initialize transport, while their mean provides fixed source-population context for the velocity field throughout integration. Minibatch OT supplies latent endpoint pairs for flow matching. At inference, the learned field transports source-seeded particles under the requested transition, and the decoder predicts expression without target cells or OT. Temporal, genetic, and drug-response models are trained separately.

# 5 Experiments 

5.1 Experimental Protocol 

We evaluate population prediction for held-out donors, unseen perturbation components or combinations, and unseen compounds. Controlled and matched comparisons assess source context and transport components. Within each task, methods share the evaluation split, gene space, sampling protocol, and evaluator. The genetic feature panel was selected before condition splitting (Section 5.3). Complete comparisons appear in Appendix A. Main comparisons use one validation-selected checkpoint per learned method and training 6setting, evaluated without retraining over three randomized test runs (seeds 42, 123, and 2026). Mean 

± sample standard deviation (SD) summarizes run-level aggregates; genetic tables report means only. Retraining SD and donor- or compound-bootstrap intervals are reported separately. Drug main-table means weight conditions equally, whereas paired intervals weight compounds equally. Appendix D.6 defines aggregation, statistical units, and uncertainty estimators. Mean L2 and effect correlation ∆r measure population mean-response agreement; Energy distance (Székely and Rizzo, 2013) and maximum mean discrepancy (MMD) (Gretton et al., 2012) compare distributions. Differential-expression (DE) recovery uses top-response-gene overlap (DE@100) or fold-change rank correlation on reference DE genes (DE ρ). Drug pEnergy and pMMD use a common projected expression space. Task-specific estimators are defined in Appendix D.6.

Validation Day 0 →Day 3 predictions select checkpoints. Requiring at least 64 cells at Days 0, 1, and 2 yields 15 evaluation donors and 30 day 0 →Day 1/2 requests. 5.5 Source-Context Information and Model Use 

The three task comparisons do not isolate source context. We next test its information content, its use by a fitted model, and its predictive benefit under matched retraining. 

5.5.1 Controlled Source Ambiguity 

We tested the information restriction in Section 3.2 using two synthetic source populations with exactly the same central cells and transition descriptor. Additional cells on the left or right distinguished the populations; their unpaired target populations required the central cells to reach opposite vertical branches. A parameter-matched no-context model replaced the pooled context with zeros. With identical shared initial states and descriptors, its deterministic field and solver produce identical shared-cell outputs for both populations. The additional source cells therefore supply the information needed to distinguish the two requests. Across three test runs of one fixed checkpoint per trained arm, branch accuracy was 50.00% without context, 98.04% with correct context, and 1.96% with swapped context (Table 4). Correct context also gave the lowest full-population Energy in this comparison. Appendix E gives the construction, evaluation protocol, and controls. 95.5.2 Donor-Context Swaps 

Using the held-out donors from Section 5.2, we split each donor’s Day 0 cells into disjoint query and context subsets to test source-context use. With query cells, requested day, model parameters, and solver fixed, we replaced only the pooled context with each of the other 14 donors’ contexts. This intervention indicates use of empirical source information by the fitted model (Appendix D.5); Section 5.6 separately tests predictive benefit under matched retraining. 

5.6 Transport and Conditioning Ablations 

Temporal transport variants. Temporal variants replace OT coupling with independent random pairing, or retain OT endpoint sampling while learning a single latent residual update. Random pairing increased mean Energy from 0.1291 to 0.1446; static OT residual increased it to 0.1350 (Table S4). Both variants also reduced ∆r and DE@100 and increased MMD. The static formulation replaces pathwise velocity regression with residual regression and omits the flow-specific ordering term (Appendix C.5). These results favor the full temporal training formulation; the static comparison does not isolate numerical integration alone. 

Source context with matched retraining. We retrained temporal models with and without source context to test predictive benefit separately from the fixed-model swaps in Section 5.5.2. Across three training seeds, the context model had lower mean MMD (0.378 vs. 0.397) and Energy (0.135 vs. 0.139), while the no-context model had higher ∆r (0.639 vs. 0.625) and DE@100 (0.529 vs. 0.515; Table S13). Context therefore favored distributional distances, whereas no context favored effect correlation and DE@100. 

Drug-conditioning variants. A matched 2 × 2 Stage-B analysis varies source-context and cell-line condi-tioning while sharing a frozen expression backbone and condition warm-start (Table S10). For Full minus No source context, the compound-weighted paired effects were 0.004 for ∆r (95% CI [0 .001 , 0.006] ) and 0.019 for DE ρ ([0 .008 , 0.033] ). Under condition weights, pEnergy slightly favored No source context and pMMD was identical at the reported precision; removing cell-line conditioning reduced ∆r from 0.425 to 0.414. These metric-specific findings concern Stage-B conditioning, separately from the main drug comparison and its test-repeat variability (Appendix C.8). 

# 6 Discussion and Conclusion 

The controlled construction identifies an information restriction when local state and the requested transition are identical. Frozen-model donor swaps show that the fitted field uses source context on biological data. Matched retraining separately tests whether supplying that information improves prediction; full-model comparisons alone do not isolate the contribution of source context. Predictive benefit depends on the setting and metric. In matched temporal retraining, context favored distributional distances, whereas the no-context model favored effect correlation and DE@100; the drug conditioning analysis also showed metric tradeoffs. The fixed-source genetic benchmark evaluates conditional perturbation prediction rather than source-context benefit. Across the biological tasks, mean-response and population-distribution gains were more consistent than covariance recovery and DE ranking. TempoBridge addresses missing source-population information in unpaired transition prediction by conditioning the transport field separately on evolving cell states, a fixed initial empirical source-population summary, and the requested transition. Temporal, genetic, and drug models are trained independently. OT 10 couplings construct the flow-matching training paths, while inference requires only the source population and transition descriptor.

# C TempoBridge Implementation Details 

C.1 Expression encoders and decoders 

Each task uses its own expression encoder and decoder. The temporal and genetic encoders remain trainable during transition learning. The drug instance first trains an expression-reconstruction backbone on the Task 3 training split and then freezes the source encoder and gene embedding during transition training; its decoder remains trainable. The temporal decoder predicts an absolute expression state, whereas the genetic and drug decoders predict source-anchored expression changes. Final expression predictions are constrained to be nonnegative. Gene-space and latent dimensions and the corresponding inference settings are listed in Table S7. Appendix D.2 gives the full temporal architecture and optimization record. 20 C.2 Descriptor encoders 

All task instances preserve the factorization introduced in Section 4: source context is computed from observed source cells, whereas the descriptor encoder receives structured information about the target condition. The temporal instance encodes the Day 0–Day 3 endpoint and supplies the requested day through the integration horizon. The genetic instance mean-pools the embeddings of the requested genes and combines the result with an intervention-type token. The drug instance encodes a 256-dimensional SMILES character n-gram vector, dose, treatment time, and cell line. Its drug-identity embedding is disabled for compound-disjoint evaluation. Appendix B gives the exact descriptor tuples and the metadata excluded from prediction. For batched computation, populations are padded and a validity mask excludes padded positions from the latent mean used as source context.

C.7 Drug adapter and evaluation interfaces 

The chemCPA adapter (Hetzel et al., 2022) uses ComPert with fixed 256-dimensional SMILES-derived features, an amortized doser, and cell-line covariates. Predictions are sampled from the predicted Gaussian expression distribution. Treatment duration is not an explicit input to this adapter. The scDFM adapter (Yu et al., 2026) uses a continuous molecular-feature, dose, duration, and cell-line interface around the PAD-Transformer backbone. The CellFlow adapter (Klein et al., 2025) uses the same types of condition inputs with an OT flow-matching model and a 50-component PCA representation fitted on training cells. All three are project adaptations, not unmodified reference benchmarks. The common evaluator receives predictions on the ordered 2,000-gene expression axis. Its distribution distances use a separate Gaussian random projection with 50 columns and seed 42; this is not CellFlow’s internal PCA space. The multiscale RBF bandwidth is calibrated on training populations. This evaluation projection is also distinct from the 128-dimensional projection used by the drug model’s covariance training loss. Adapter-specific generation procedures are retained; outputs are not forced to share a common sampling model.

# D Temporal Differentiation Details 

D.1 Dataset and preprocessing 

The data split contained 21,533 training cells from 77 donors, 3,556 validation cells from 17 donors, and 10,955 cells from 32 donors in the evaluation partition. Requiring at least 64 cells at each of Days 0, 1, and 2 yielded the fixed 15-donor evaluation cohort used by every method. Cells were retained if they had at least 500 total counts, 200 detected genes, and a mitochondrial fraction below 0.2; genes had to occur in at least three cells. Counts were normalized to a library size of 10,000 and transformed with log(1 + x). Highly variable genes were selected without using the evaluation cohort. The nine prespecified genes were added to the 2,000-gene set before model training. 

D.2 Architecture and optimization 

The expression encoder has widths 2009 → 1024 → 256 ; the decoder mirrors this mapping with a Softplus output. The velocity MLP uses two hidden transformations after a 769-dimensional concatenated input. The complete model contains 9,797,593 parameters. Three-seed training used weights 1.0, 0.1, 0.1, and 0.1 for flow matching, source reconstruction, target reconstruction, and trajectory consistency, respectively. The trajectory term evaluated virtual target times {1, 2, 3} with a ranking margin of 0.02; it did not use measured Day 1 or Day 2 expression. Optimization used AdamW with a base learning rate of 2 × 10 −4, a learning 26 rate of 2 × 10 −5 for pretrained parameters, weight decay 0.005, gradient clipping at 1.0, a batch size of 256, and bfloat16 arithmetic. Mean training cost was 2.467 ± 0.896 GPU-hours. With 32-step Heun integration, inference took 0.032 ± 0.001 seconds per population or 0.374 ± 0.170 seconds per 1,000 cells in the timing experiment. Checkpoint selection used validation Day 0 →Day 3 endpoints. Models were evaluated every 500 steps and selected by raw single-bandwidth RBF MMD, with patience 8 after two warm-up evaluations and a minimum improvement of 0.001. The selected steps for seeds 42, 123, and 2026 were 8,500, 10,500, and 4,000. These records identify independently trained checkpoint candidates; the main comparison uses one selected checkpoint for all three test repeats. Main-table uncertainty follows the fixed-model randomized-test-run definition in Section 5.1, rather than treating checkpoint identifiers as test-repeat identifiers. 

D.3 Representation and gene-space sensitivity 

The temporal task was rescored on the full 2,009-gene training space, the subset of 2,000 highly variable genes, and the nine-gene prespecified panel. This analysis checks whether the conclusion depends on one favorable feature set. A scratch-encoder control was also trained for three seeds. The pretrained initialization did not produce a stable advantage and is not presented as a contribution.

# E Controlled Source-Context Ambiguity 

E.1 Controlled construction 

The experiment is confined to a two-dimensional latent space and uses identity encoders and decoders. For each replicate, contexts A and B contain the exact same 128 shared source coordinates sampled from 

N ((0 , 0) , 0.15 2I). Each also contains 128 context-specific source points, centered at (−2, 0) for A and (2 , 0) 

for B. The corresponding shared target components are sampled independently around (0 , 2) and (0 , −2) ,whereas the context-specific target components remain centered at (−2, 0) and (2 , 0) . Each source and target population has 256 points. Targets are shuffled after generation, and component labels are retained only for analysis. Both contexts receive the fixed descriptor c = (0 , 0) .The generator uses 64 training, 16 validation, and 32 test replicates per context. Within a replicate, the maximum absolute difference between the A/B shared source supports is exactly zero.The target branches differ only in the population associated with those shared states. Their ideal centers are separated by four latent units. 32 E.2 Training protocol 

The experiment directly instantiates OTCFMTransition with latent and condition dimensions of 2, hidden width 128, no dropout, LayerNorm, and no BatchNorm. All trainable arms contain 18,050 parameters. Training uses the linear flow path, an entropic Sinkhorn coupling with ε = 0 .05 and 50 iterations, 64 OT cells per population, and batches of 16 populations. The objective is flow-matching MSE only. AdamW uses learning rate 0.002, weight decay 10 −5, and gradient clipping at 1.0. The maximum training length is 2,500 steps. Validation is performed every 250 steps using mean full-population Energy over validation replicates. Early stopping begins after 500 steps, uses patience 6, and requires an improvement of 10 −4. Test predictions use a 32-step Heun solver with no initial noise. Each trained arm contributes one validation-selected checkpoint, which is held fixed across three test runs. No retraining or weight updates occur between test runs. Hyperparameters were fixed before training, and no post-result tuning was performed. 

E.3 Experimental arms 

The descriptor-only arm retains the same model and source particles as TempoBridge but sets the pooled source-context slot to zero. TempoBridge uses the mean of all 256 empirical source particles; the 64-point OT subsample does not redefine this context. Swapped context uses the frozen TempoBridge checkpoint and exchanges A/B empirical context vectors only at inference, without retraining. The oracle arm is trained with fixed vectors (−1, 0) and (1 , 0) for contexts A and B; it provides a diagnostic reference with explicit context labels. 

E.4 Evaluation metrics and test-run aggregation 

For each test replicate, Dctx is the mean Euclidean distance between the two predicted endpoints of each identical shared source point under contexts A and B. Shared error is the mean of the distances from the predicted shared-component centroids to the ideal centers (0 , 2) and (0 , −2) . Branch accuracy counts a shared prediction as correct when its second coordinate is strictly positive for A or strictly negative for B, then averages over shared points and contexts; zero is counted as incorrect. Energy evaluates the full predicted and target populations. Table 4 reports the mean and sample SD across three test runs of one fixed checkpoint per trained arm. Each run contributes one aggregate score per metric. The sample SD uses the three run-level scores and denominator 3 − 1; it describes test-run variability conditional on the selected checkpoint. The swapped-context setting uses the same TempoBridge checkpoint as the correct-context setting, changing only the context input.
