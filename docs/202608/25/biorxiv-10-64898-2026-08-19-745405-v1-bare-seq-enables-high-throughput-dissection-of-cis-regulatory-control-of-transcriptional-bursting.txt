Abstract 16 

Transcriptional bursts determine RNA output through two kinetic parameters: burst 17 

size and burst frequency. How cis-regulatory DNA encodes these kinetic parameters 18 

remains unclear, in part because existing approaches do not combine scalable 19 

sequence perturbation with allele-resolved burst inference. Here, we developed Bulk 20 

Allele Resolution Sequencing (BARe-seq), an allele-resolved massively parallel 21 

reporter assay that enables inference of transcriptional burst parameters from bulk 22 

sequencing. Applying BARe-seq to libraries of 1000 promoters and 1000 enhancers in 23 

Drosophila S2 cells revealed distinct kinetic properties of promoters and enhancers. 24 

Promoter-dependent mean expression was driven by both burst size and burst 25 

frequency: TATA-box promoters showed larger bursts, whereas DPE promoters 26 

showed higher burst frequency. In contrast, enhancer strength was primarily driven by 27 

burst frequency, although specific transcription factor motifs were also associated with 28 

burst size. Thus, BARe-seq dissects cis-regulatory control of transcriptional bursting 29 

and extends allele-resolved measurements to pooled reporter assays in bulk 30 

sequencing experiments. 31

The transcriptional output is determined by both the number of transcripts produced 41 

per active period (burst size) and the occurrence rate of these periods (burst 42 

frequency) (reviewed in Nicolas et al., 2017; Raj & van Oudenaarden, 2008). While 43 

regulatory inputs modulate gene expression by changing one or both of these 44 

parameters, it remains unclear how specific cis-regulatory DNA sequences are linked 45 

to burst kinetics. Previous work has suggested a division between promoter and 46 

enhancer contributions. Promoter architecture has been primarily associated with burst 47 

size, including links between TATA-containing promoters and larger, but not more 48 

frequent, bursts (Hornung et al., 2012; Larsson et al., 2019; Pimmett et al., 2021), while 49 

enhancer activity is associated primarily with burst frequency (Falo-Sanjuan et al., 50 

2019; Fukaya et al., 2016; Larsson et al., 2019; Hoppe et al., 2020). 51 

52 

Yet, perturbations of individual promoter motifs (Yokoshi et al., 2022), as well as 53 

changes in signaling, transcription factor abundance, residence time or binding affinity 54 

can affect both burst size and frequency (Senecal et al., 2014; Ochiai et al., 2020). 55 

These observations indicate that the cis-regulatory encoding of burst kinetics extends 56 

beyond a simple model in which promoters determine burst size and enhancers 57 

determine burst frequency. However, it remains underexplored how promoter and 58 

enhancer sequences—particularly their motif compositions—relate to bursting. 59 

Resolving this complexity requires assays that can evaluate whether regulatory 60 

sequence differences are reflected in burst size, burst frequency, or both. 61 

62 

Defining general rules that link cis-regulatory sequence to burst kinetics requires 63 

scaling these measurements. Existing methods address different aspects of this 64 

problem but fail to combine burst-parameter inference with systematic sequence 65 

dissection: Imaging-based approaches measure transcription dynamics directly but 66 

are limited in throughput and typically require dedicated reporter engineering (reviewed 67 

in Nicolas et al., 2017). Allele-resolved single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) has 68 

enabled transcriptome-wide inference of bursting in endogenous contexts (Larsson et 69 

al., 2019, 2021; Ochiai et al., 2020), but systematic cis-regulatory perturbations remain 70 

difficult to test at endogenous loci. Conversely, massively parallel reporter assays 71 

(MPRAs) offer the scalability to assess thousands of sequences, including perturbed 72

To address this methodological gap, we developed Bulk Allele-Resolution sequencing 78 

(BARe-seq). BARe-seq uses plasmid barcodes to achieve single-allele resolution from 79 

a bulk experiment. By tracking of transcripts back to uniquely barcoded plasmids, the 80 

assay isolates independent transcriptional activity of individual template copies within 81 

a pooled sample. We applied this strategy for high-throughput inference of burst 82 

parameters across large libraries of cis-regulatory elements and quantified burst 83 

parameters across libraries of 1000 promoters and 1000 enhancers in Drosophila S2 84 

cells. Using BARe-seq, we recovered established relationships between promoter 85 

motifs and bursting, including the association of TATA-containing promoters with larger 86 

bursts, and identified additional motif relationships involving DPE (downstream 87 

promoter element), Inr (Initiator), E-box and Ohler6 motifs. We further showed that 88 

promoters and enhancers contribute asymmetrically to transcriptional bursting: 89 

promoter-dependent mean expression variation is globally driven by both burst size 90 

and frequency changes, while enhancer-dependent variation is primarily driven by 91 

burst frequency changes. Yet, beneath this global trend, we found that specific 92 

transcription factor motifs in enhancers are associated with burst size and frequency 93 

and used designed enhancer variants to test how motif identities and the enhancer-94 

sequence context contribute to bursting. Together, our results establish BARe-seq as 95 

a scalable approach for dissecting how cis-regulatory sequences encode 96 

transcriptional bursting kinetics. 97

BARe-seq uses plasmid barcoding and transcript UMIs to count the mRNA output per 100 

plasmid 101 

BARe-seq uses high-complexity plasmid barcodes to recover transcript counts from 102 

individual reporter plasmids in bulk sequencing experiments. By introducing random 103 

DNA barcodes (plasmid-specific unique molecular identifiers = pUMIs) into pooled 104 

libraries of regulatory elements, we generated a library of effectively uniquely barcoded 105 

plasmids, allowing each expressed RNA molecule to be assigned to its plasmid of 106 

origin. During sample processing, each RNA molecule is additionally associated with 107

The promoter BARe-seq library is labeled with effectively unique pUMIs 117 

To establish BARe-seq in the context of promoters, we selected 1000 deeply 118 

characterized Drosophila S2 cell promoters spanning a broad range of activities 119 

measured by the promoter activity reporter assay self-transcribing active core 120 

promoter sequencing (STAP-seq) (see Methods) (Haberle et al., 2019). We cloned 121 

these promoters as a promoter-BARe-seq library in which each plasmid carried a 20-122 

nucleotide random barcode that served as a pUMI (Figure 1A, Supplemental Figure 123 

1A). Next-generation sequencing confirmed the high complexity of the plasmid pool: 124 

among 10 7 sequenced promoter – pUMI combinations, 98.3% were unique, making 125 

barcode collision within the assay highly unlikely so that the pUMIs successfully 126 

resolve single plasmids (Supplemental Figure 1B). 127 

128 

BARe-seq recovers promoter activities and produces reproducible per-plasmid 129 

expression distributions 130 

We next asked whether BARe-seq accurately recapitulates prior measurements of 131 

promoter activity (Arnold et al., 2017; Haberle et al., 2019). We placed the promoter-132 

BARe-seq library under the control of the highly active zfh1 enhancer, transfected it 133 

into Drosophila S2 cells, and quantified reporter expression by bulk next-generation 134 

sequencing (see Methods). At the aggregate level – by collapsing reads across all 135 

pUMIs – BARe-seq activity scores were highly correlated with previously published 136 

STAP-seq data (Pearson’s correlation coefficient R = 0.94) (Haberle et al., 2019). This 137 

confirmed that pUMI insertion did not disrupt reporter performance and that BARe-seq 138 

captured promoter activities (Figure 1B). Concurrently, because each sequencing read 139 

contained the promoter identity, pUMI, and tUMI, reporter RNA molecules could be 140 

accurately counted at the level of individual plasmids (Figure 1A, Supplemental Figure 141 

1C). This yielded a distribution of reporter transcripts per plasmid for each promoter. 142

Burst parameters can be inferred from per-plasmid transcript distributions using the 148 

two-state model of stochastic transcription 149 

The dynamics of gene expression are captured by the two-state model of stochastic 150 

transcription described by Peccoud and Ycart (1995) (Peccoud & Ycart, 1995) 151 

(Supplemental Figure 2A). Assuming predominant bursting behavior, the stationary 152 

state of this model is described by a negative binomial distribution with parameters 153 

mean 

μ and dispersion r (Raj et al., 2006; Grün et al., 2014). In this limit, dispersion r154 

equates to burst frequency, while the ratio of 

μ/r defines burst size. To ensure accurate 155 

model fitting, we applied a Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) goodness-of-fit threshold ( p >156 

0.01) between empirical and inferred distributions, retaining data for 547 promoters 157 

from replicate 1 and 593 promoters from replicate 2. The inferred parameters proved 158 

reproducible across independent experimental replicates ( R = 0.82 for burst size, R =159 

0.66 for burst frequency, and R = 0.75 for mean expression) (Supplemental Figure 160 

2B). To further improve the robustness and reproducibility, we pooled count data from 161 

two replicates and inferred the burst parameters for 608 promoters. We further 162 

restricted downstream analyses to promoters above an expression threshold guided 163 

by the observed bimodal distribution of means, where parameter estimates are more 164 

robust ( R = 0.92 for burst size, R = 0.74 for burst frequency, and R = 0.9 for mean 165 

expression) resulting in a total of 492 promoters with confidently inferred burst 166 

parameters (Supplemental Figure 2C-E). The inferred parameters captured a broad 167 

dynamic range, with burst size, frequency and mean expression each spanning nearly 168 

two orders of magnitude (Figure 1D). 169

BARe-seq recovers the established association between TATA box-containing 181 

promoters and larger bursts 182 

We first asked whether BARe-seq recovers established relationships between 183 

promoter sequence and burst kinetics. Prior studies in yeast, Drosophila and 184 

mammalian cells have consistently linked TATA box-containing promoters to 185 

increased burst size (Hornung et al., 2012; Larsson et al., 2019; Pimmett et al., 2021; 186 

Yokoshi et al., 2022). Our promoter library represents characteristic Drosophila 187 

promoter types in terms of core promoter motifs and motif combinations, including 188 

TATA box-containing promoters (Figure 2A, Supplemental Figure 3A). Consistent with 189 

previous work, TATA box-containing promoters associated with higher burst sizes and 190 

lower burst frequencies than promoters without a TATA box. This relationship 191 

persisted even when accounting for differences in mean expression (Supplemental 192 

Figure 3B). 193 

194 

TATA and DPE show distinct motif-presence and motif-strength relationships with 195 

burst parameters 196 

To extend the analysis, we compared the TATA box against the downstream promoter 197 

element (DPE). Both are prominent Drosophila developmental core promoter motifs 198 

but occur in a largely mutually exclusive manner (Ohler, 2006; Arnold et al., 2017). As 199 

expected, the TATA box-containing promoters associated with higher burst size and 200 

lower frequency, while DPE-containing promoters showed higher burst frequencies but 201 

no significant difference in burst size (Figure 2B). Having observed these motif 202 

associations, we next asked whether motif affinity, approximated by the position weight 203 

matrix (PWM) score, was quantitatively related to burst parameters. The TATA box 204 

PWM score correlated positively with burst size ( R = 0.59, p = 3.02*10 -17 ) and 205 

negatively with burst frequency ( R = -0.21, p = 0.0054). Conversely, the DPE PWM 206 

score correlated positively with burst frequency ( R = 0.24, p = 0.0004) and negatively 207 

with burst size ( R = -0.15, p = 0.035) (Figure 2C). Therefore, BARe-seq resolves motif-208 

burst relationships down to the quantitative level of motif affinity. 209

Promoter motif–burst relationships generalize across enhancer contexts 211 

To determine whether the relationships between promoter types and burst parameters 212 

persist across enhancer contexts, we performed the promoter-BARe-seq screen with 213 

two additional enhancers (CG4822 and Pld). These enhancers were selected based 214 

on their activity in a previous self-transcribing active regulatory region sequencing 215 

(STARR-seq) screen (Zabidi et al., 2015) and confirmed by luciferase assays 216 

(Supplemental Figure 3C,D). We inferred burst parameters for each dataset separately 217 

and subsequently applied multivariable regression to assess motif associations with 218 

the parameters while accounting for motif co-occurrence (Figure 2D, Supplemental 219 

Figure 3E). Across the three enhancer contexts, the TATA box consistently associated 220 

with higher burst size and lower burst frequency, while the DPE motif associated with 221 

higher burst frequency. In contrast, E-box, Inr and Ohler6 associated significantly with 222 

higher burst frequency only in individual enhancer contexts, suggesting their kinetic 223 

contributions may depend on enhancer mediated mechanisms. To summarize global 224 

motif effects, we integrated the regression coefficients and standard errors by meta-225 

analysis (Figure 2E). This analysis identified the TATA box as the only significant 226 

positive predictor of burst size, while DPE, E-box, Inr and Ohler6 motifs were significant 227 

positive predictors of burst frequency. Therefore, the core promoter motifs encode 228 

distinct but generalizable kinetic features that are robust across enhancer contexts. 229

Promoter-dependent expression variation is driven by both burst size and frequency 231 

Having validated promoter-BARe-seq by recovering kinetic signatures for core 232 

promoter motifs, we next asked how the interplay between promoters and enhancers 233 

regulates bursting. The functional interactions between the two regulatory elements 234 

are central to gene regulation, but it remains unclear how their respective contributions 235 

to transcription are driven by burst size and burst frequency. BARe-seq allowed us to 236 

decouple the effects of promoters and enhancers by varying one element while holding 237 

the other constant. We first analyzed promoter-dependent variation in expression in 238 

the three promoter-BARe-seq screens described above (Figure 2), which assess the 239 

1000-promoter library against three constant enhancers. We ordered the promoters by 240 

mean expression from weak to strong and observed that both burst size and burst 241 

frequency increased with mean expression, a trend that was true for all three enhancer 242 

contexts (Figure 3A). Linear model slopes were positive for both parameters across all 243 

three contexts (Figure 3C), indicating that promoter-dependent differences in 244 

expression are driven by changes in both burst size and burst frequency. 245

Enhancer-dependent expression variation is reflected primarily in burst frequency 247 

To assess enhancer-dependent variation in expression, we inverted the experimental 248 

design by coupling a constant promoter to a library of 1000 well-characterized 249 

enhancers, adapting the STARR-seq design (Arnold et al., 2013; de Almeida et al., 250 

2022). We measured enhancer activity with DSCP, a synthetic developmental core 251 

promoter, and additionally with two highly active housekeeping-type promoters, Rpl35 252 

and Rps15a (Supplemental Figure 4A)(Pfeiffer et al., 2008; Arnold et al., 2013; Zabidi 253 

et al., 2015). Aggregate enhancer-BARe-seq enhancer activity correlated well with the 254 

previously published STARR-seq measurements ( R = 0.84) (de Almeida et al., 2022) 255 

(Supplemental Figure 4B). After pooling replicates and applying the same quality 256 

thresholds used for the promoter analysis, burst parameters were inferred for 451 257 

enhancers with DSCP, 685 with Rpl35 and 601 with Rps15a ( KS p-value > 0.01 and 258 

mean expression threshold; Supplemental Figure 4C). The enhancers showed a wide 259 

range of mean expression and burst parameters comparable to the promoters 260 

(Supplemental Figure 4D), allowing us to order enhancers by mean expression from 261 

weak to strong. In contrast to promoters, only burst frequency but not burst size 262 

increased with enhancer strength (mean expression). Linear model slopes were only 263 

positive for burst frequency but weakly negative for burst size (Figure 3B,C). Thus, 264 

enhancer-dependent differences in mean expression were driven primarily by burst 265 

frequency, not burst size. Together, these data indicate that promoters and enhancers 266 

contribute asymmetrically to burst kinetics. Across promoters, mean expression was 267 

driven by both burst size and frequency, yet across enhancers, mean expression 268 

changes were primarily controlled by changes in burst frequency. 269

Synthetic motif insertions probe the causal effects of individual motifs on bursting 289 

While multivariable regression establishes motif associations, sequence perturbations 290 

are used to test causality. Previous studies have shown that motif deletions and 291 

insertions can alter enhancer activity in S2 cells (Yáñez-Cuna et al., 2014; de Almeida 292 

et al., 2022). To determine whether adding specific enhancer motifs alters mean 293 

expression via changes in burst size, frequency, or both, we generated synthetic 294 

enhancer variants by inserting single motif copies into existing enhancers at positions 295 

predicted to be not essential for enhancer activity (non-disruptive positions) (Methods 296 

and Figure 4C, Supplemental Figure 5D)). We obtained results from 15 wild-type 297 

enhancers spanning a range of burst sizes, burst frequencies and mean expression 298 

values (Supplemental Figure 5E). Replicate measurements of the insertion constructs 299 

were well correlated (N=2; burst size: R = 0.89, burst frequency: R = 0.71, mean 300 

expression: R = 0.9, Supplemental Figure 5F) and allowed burst parameter inference 301 

on average for 73% of the enhancers per motif. For each motif, we then compared 302 

mean expression, burst size and burst frequency for each enhancer before and after 303 

motif insertion, and then grouped relative changes in mean expression, burst size, and 304 

burst frequency by the type of inserted motif. 305

Discussion 321 

BARe-seq combines the scalability and sequence perturbability of MPRAs with the 322 

allelic resolution required to infer transcriptional burst parameters. In doing so, it 323 

bridges the gap between imaging-based measurements of transcriptional dynamics, 324 

allele-resolved scRNA-seq, and conventional aggregate reporter assays. Because 325 

BARe-seq extracts allele-resolved count distributions from a pooled bulk sample, it 326 

bypasses the technical noise and cell-to-cell variability of scRNA-seq experiments 327 

(Rosales-Alvarez et al., 2023). By overcoming these technical bottlenecks, we were 328 

able to map cis-regulatory sequence to function for burst size and frequency in high-329 

throughput. 330 

331 

Applying BARe-seq to the systematic analysis of promoter-enhancer combinations 332 

revealed an asymmetry between promoters and enhancers. Across promoter libraries, 333 

mean expression correlated with both burst size and burst frequency. Across enhancer 334 

libraries, mean expression varied predominantly with burst frequency. Promoter motif 335 

composition was also associated with burst parameters across different enhancer 336 

contexts: the presence of a TATA box was linked with higher burst size, while DPE, E-337 

box, Inr and Ohler6 were associated with higher burst frequency. These sequence 338 

dependencies extend previous observations from individual Drosophila and human 339 

promoters that linked the DPE or Inr motif to elevated burst frequency (Hendy et al., 340 

2017; Yokoshi et al., 2022) and are consistent with biochemical studies showing how 341 

core promoters differentially engage the core transcriptional machinery (Serebreni et 342 

al., 2023). For example, TAF1 (TATA-associated factor 1), a TFIID subunit implicated 343 

in the negative regulation of burst size, associates more strongly with DPE promoters 344 

(Pennington et al., 2013; Serebreni et al., 2023). This biochemical bias is in line with 345 

our observation that DPE promoters exhibit an inverse kinetic signature to TATA box-346 

containing promoters linking them to higher burst frequency and lower burst size. 347 

Our enhancer analyses clarify that enhancer function cannot be reduced to burst 348 

frequency alone. At the level of overall enhancer activity, differences in mean 349 

expression tracked primarily with burst frequency, consistent with previous studies 350

distinction aligns with the observation that different transcription factors binding the 360 

enhancer either affect burst frequency or both parameters simultaneously (Keller et 361 

al., 2020). Furthermore, this may explain why previous reports have linked signaling 362 

inputs which affected enhancer activity to both burst size and burst frequency (Lee et 363 

al., 2019; Ochiai et al., 2020; Senecal et al., 2014). 364 

365 

As an MPRA, BARe-seq simplifies regulatory complexity and enables testing of 366 

sequence logic. Each construct tests one enhancer-promoter pair at a fixed distance, 367 

allowing sequence effects to be compared independent of the endogenous chromatin 368 

context. Burst parameters are inferred from sequencing-derived steady-state 369 

distributions rather than measured directly in live cells. The inference therefore 370 

depends on the assumptions of a simplified two-state model (Raj et al., 2006), even 371 

though additional promoter states and more complex kinetic schemes have been 372 

proposed (Tunnacliffe & Chubb, 2020). 373 

374 

Ultimately, the scalability and conceptual simplicity of BARe-seq make it transferable 375 

to other biological questions and assays. By applying this strategy to transcriptional 376 

bursting, we demonstrated that resolving transcript counts on a per-template basis 377 

allows the high-throughput mathematical separation of burst size from burst frequency. 378 

The method’s core innovation – achieving allele resolution from a bulk experiment via 379 

unique barcoding – is broadly applicable to other plasmid-based assays and questions 380 

that require single-allele resolution. 381

Generating libraries 723 

300 ‑bp oligonucleotides containing 133-bp (promoter) or 249 ‑bp (enhancer) candidate 724 

sequences were custom synthesized by Twist Biocence. Oligos were amplified and 725 

prepared for cloning by sequential PCR steps using KAPA HiFi HotStart ReadyMix 726 

(Roche Diagnostics), with bead-based clean ups (AMPure XP Beads, Beckman 727 

Coulter A63881) between steps to remove residual primers (Supplemental Figure 1A). 728 

All primer sequences are provided in Supplemental Table 1. First starting each oligo 729 

pool was amplified with pool-specific primers from 1ng of template DNA within the 730 

linear amplification range. Second, plasmid UMIS (pUMIs) were introduced using 731 

single-primer linear PCR (50 cycles) using 2.5ng of template per 50 μl PCR reaction, 732 

with 20 reactions to maintain complexity. Third, Gibson assembly overhangs were 733 

added by PCR. PCR cycle numbers were determined empirically to ensure that this 734 

amplification was also in a linear range. 735 

The promoter and enhancer of the published reporter vectors for STAP- and STARR-736

Motif addition: To test the impact of motif addition on enhancer, we designed motif-787

MPRA data analysis 852 

A custom index of the designed oligonucleotide sequences of each library was 853 

generated using the buildindex function from the Rsubread R package version 2.10.0 854 

(Liao et al 2019). Paired-end reads were aligned using the align function using type = 855 

“dna”, unique = TRUE, maxMismatches = 1 for 36 or 50bp reads and 3 for 150bp 856 

sequencing reads, nTrim = 1. Oligos with coverage below 2% of the median library 857 

cpverage and reads containing poly-G stretches longer than 10bp were discarded. To 858 

reduce overcounting from sequencing errors, pUMIs and transcript UMIs (tUMIs) were 859 

collapsed sequentially. Reads were first grouped by oligo identifier, and exact pUMI– 860 

tUMI combinations were deduplicated. Within each oligo, pUMIs were sorted by RNA 861 

count, and lower-abundance pUMIs within a Levenshtein distance of three from a 862 

higher-abundance pUMI were collapsed using stringdist version 0.9.8. After pUMI 863 

correction, tUMIs were collapsed using a Hamming distance of one, as described 864 

previously (de Almeida et al., 2022). After collapsing we discarded reads where the 865 

fixed part of the pUMI did not match the expected sequence structure and then 866 

generated tables of transcript counts per plasmid, which were subsequently used for 867 

burst parameter inference. Activity scores for each oligo were estimated as log2 fold-868 

change of RNA counts relative to plasmid DNA input using DESeq2 (Love et al., 2014), 869 

following de Almeida et al. (2022). 870

Multivariable analysis 931 

Associations between motif composition and the three burst parameters were 932 

quantified using multivariable linear regression. For each dataset and burst parameter 933 

- log10-transformed burst size, burst frequency or mean expression was modeled as 934 

a function of motif counts. While promoter motifs were either absent or only present 935 

once per sequence, motif counts in enhancers were clipped at two or three 936 

occurrences (Supplemental Figure 5) before fitting to reduce the influence of high-937 

count outliers. All selected motifs were included simultaneously in the regression 938 

model using lm in R. Regression coefficients therefore represent conditional 939 

associations between each motif and the readout, controlling for the other motifs 940 

included in the model. 941

Meta-analysis 943 

For each motif–outcome combination, regression coefficients and standard errors from 944 

the multivariable linear models were pooled across datasets (promoters in three 945 

enhancer contexts) using inverse-variance weighted fixed-effect meta-analysis using 946 

the R metafor package version 3.4-0 . Only motif–outcome combinations represented 947 

in all three datasets were included. Pooled estimates were reported on the log10 scale 948 

and converted to fold changes for interpretation; 95% confidence intervals and p-949 

values were derived from the pooled meta-analysis. Motifs were classified as 950 

significant if the pooled association reached p<0.05 for either burst size or burst 951 

frequency. 952
